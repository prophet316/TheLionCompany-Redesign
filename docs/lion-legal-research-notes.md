# Lion website legal-page research notes

Checked: 2026-08-08
Scope: ordinary United States nonprofit website practice; operational research, not legal advice.

## Current sources reviewed

- Federal Trade Commission, COPPA FAQ: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions
- Texas Attorney General, Texas Data Privacy and Security Act overview: https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/es/node/259071
- Texas Business & Commerce Code Chapter 541: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/BC/pdf/BC.541.pdf
- Farella Braun + Martel, nonprofit privacy-policy practices: https://www.fbm.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity/publications/privacy-policy-best-practices-for-nonprofits/
- Farella Braun + Martel, nonprofit website terms practices: https://www.fbm.com/exempt-organizations/publications/nonprofit-websites-and-terms-of-use-best-practices-and-common-pitfalls/

## Findings applied

- The FTC encourages nonprofits to publish clear privacy policies and to extend child-privacy protections even though nonprofits generally are outside COPPA's commercial-operator scope.
- FTC guidance emphasizes plainly identifying the operator, collected information, uses and disclosures, parent/request procedures, data minimization, security, retention, and deletion.
- The Texas Data Privacy and Security Act currently exempts nonprofit organizations, but transparent collection, purpose, provider, security, access, correction, deletion, and opt-out language remains ordinary responsible practice.
- Nonprofit website practice supports terms that identify site scope, ministry/informational boundaries, acceptable use, intellectual-property treatment, third-party links, availability, disclaimers, liability limits, governing law, changes, severability, and contact information.
